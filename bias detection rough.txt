That is a reasonable direction. Your guide’s preference gives us a usable research domain, while the earlier assessment gives us a way to avoid building another generic “biased/unbiased news classifier.”

The field should be fixed as:

Bias detection in news and media

But the technical problem should be narrowed to:

Editorial Transformation Provenance

Detect, classify, and attribute framing changes introduced when a common source record is transformed into a headline, quotation, summary, caption, or published news article.

This is different from predicting whether an outlet is left, centre, or right.

Why generic media-bias detection should be excluded

Existing systems already analyze:

Political lean
Tone
Topic selection
Sentence-level framing
Publisher-level patterns
Quotes
Event clusters
Headline faithfulness
Text-image relationships
Multimodal bias

The published Media Bias Detector performs sentence-, article-, and publisher-level analysis of selection and framing bias. Its annotations include political lean, tone, topics, article type, events, quotes, and sentence focus.

The MediaSpin Dataset contains 78,910 pairs of headlines edited after publication and annotations covering 13 bias categories, including sensationalism, spin, omission, and slant.

FIFO evaluates framing in machine-generated news summaries using jury-annotated and expert-labelled examples. This means summary-level framing and omission are also active research areas.

Therefore, our research should not be:

Input: one article
Output: biased or unbiased


It should instead be:

Input:
Authoritative or common source record
+
One or more published transformations

Output:
Which information was preserved, omitted, weakened, strengthened,
reordered, selectively quoted, misattributed, or reframed

1. Hierarchical source alignment

Article sentences should not be matched through embedding similarity alone.

The alignment layer is necessary because one article sentence may combine several source sentences, while one source claim may be distributed across multiple article sentences.

2. Discourse-aware context model

The model should explicitly identify:

NEGATION
QUALIFICATION
CONDITION
UNCERTAINTY
CORRECTION
CONCESSION
CAUSAL_EXPLANATION
TEMPORAL_LIMIT
SOURCE_ATTRIBUTION


Example:

Source:
“We do not expect prices to fall immediately, although the policy
may reduce inflation over the next two quarters.”

Headline:
“Policy will reduce prices.”


The important transformation is not ordinary text difference. It includes:

Removal of negation
Removal of timing
Change from uncertainty to certainty
Change from inflation to prices

A generic sentiment classifier will not capture this reliably.

3. Signed semantic-strength transformation

The method needs to identify direction.

Examples:

may → will                strengthening
some → all                strengthening
associated with → causes  strengthening
likely → confirmed        strengthening
will → may                weakening
all → some                weakening


A candidate semantic-strength value can be defined as:

ΔS=Spublished−Ssource\Delta S = S_{\text{published}} - S_{\text{source}}

The model must retain the sign:

Positive: strengthened claim
Negative: weakened claim
Zero: approximately preserved


The ordering should be learned or constrained separately for:

Modality
Quantification
Causality
Certainty
Attribution
Temporal extent
4. Context-loss score

For a quotation qq, investigate:

CLS(q)=wnNq+wmMq+wcCq+waAq+wtTqCLS(q)= w_nN_q+ w_mM_q+ w_cC_q+ w_aA_q+ w_tT_q

Where:

NqN_q: negation loss
MqM_q: modality or qualification loss
CqC_q: correction or concession loss
AqA_q: attribution inconsistency
TqT_q: temporal-context loss

This should not be introduced as the invention until experiments establish:

Which terms are necessary
How weights are estimated
Whether the score generalizes
Whether a simpler baseline performs equally well
5. Transformation provenance rather than one bias score

The output should look like:

Transformation ID: TR-0024

Source:
“The preliminary survey suggests that some districts may require
additional assistance.”

Published:

Transformations:

Human interpretation required: YES


The system detects measurable transformations. It does not declare the journalist or publisher biased.

This boundary is important for scientific validity and fairness.

Dataset strategy

Use three dataset layers.

Layer 1: Controlled synthetic transformations

Start from verified source text and programmatically apply:

Negation removal
Modality strengthening
Quantifier change
Attribution removal
Sentence reordering
Qualification deletion
Selective quotation
Correction deletion

This establishes exact transformation ground truth.

Synthetic examples must remain separate from real-media evaluation.

Layer 2: Existing media datasets

NewsMediaBias-Plus includes more than 40,000 text-image samples with media-bias annotations and metadata. It can be used for external comparison, but it is not sufficient for source-to-publication provenance because the dataset is designed primarily around bias-labelled media content.

MediaSpin is particularly relevant for evaluating editorial transformations because it contains before-and-after headline revisions.

Layer 3: Manually aligned source-article corpus

Create a smaller high-quality corpus containing:

Official source
Published article
Headline
Claims
Quotes
Source spans
Transformation labels
Annotator agreement


Prefer sources with clear provenance:

Court judgments and reporting
Government press releases and reporting
Parliamentary or legislative transcripts and reporting
Company regulatory filings and reporting
Public speeches and reporting
Research-paper abstracts and news reporting

Avoid beginning with political-left versus political-right labels.

Validation
Split by event

Articles covering the same event cannot appear across training and test sets.

Split by publisher

Hold out publishers to test whether the method learned transformations rather than publisher identity.

Split by source type

Train on statements and transcripts, then test separately on reports or judgments.

Split by transformation

Hold out one transformation category to test unknown-operation rejection.
